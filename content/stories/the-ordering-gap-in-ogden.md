Ogden's food scene runs on history — a downtown built around Historic 25th
Street, where a century-old storefront is as likely to hold a taqueria as a
gastropub. What it doesn't run on, for most of its independents, is any
ordering channel they own.

In [my verified catalogue](/stories/the-online-ordering-gap) of the Wasatch
Front's independents, Ogden counts 74. **46 of them — 62% — have no direct
online-ordering channel**: their online orders, where they exist at all,
happen through marketplace listings at 15–30% commission or on
[DoorDash-hosted Storefront pages](/stories/doordash-storefront) (12 of the
46). That's well above the corridor's 57%.

Two distinctions make Ogden's gap unusual. It has **14 independents with no
website at all** — the most of any city on the Front; for nearly one in
three of its no-channel restaurants, a hungry phone finds no menu, no hours,
no page. And it holds **two of the corridor's 17 verified zero-online
restaurants** — kitchens with no way to take an online order anywhere,
confirmed against every platform.

## Established, reviewed, invisible

Ogden's version of the gap follows the corridor pattern: these are proven
kitchens, not marginal ones. Forty-two of the 46 have more than a hundred
Google reviews; the group averages about 975 reviews at 4.4 stars. The
cuisine mix skews exactly the way the
[corridor data](/stories/the-online-ordering-gap) does — Ogden's independents
are led by 16 Mexican restaurants, with Chinese and Thai close behind, the
family-run kitchens least likely to have spare evenings for web software.

## The 28 who've solved it

On the other side, 28 Ogden independents run their own ordering, and the
vendor mix mirrors [the corridor landscape](/stories/what-utah-independents-actually-use)
in miniature: Toast leads with 10, then Square, SpotOn, and Olo with 3
each. Ordering tech clearly *works* in Ogden — for the restaurants whose
volume justifies a monthly software bill. The 46 without are
disproportionately the budget spots the subscription model screens out.

## What it costs

Take a modest Ogden restaurant doing $3,000 a month through delivery apps at
25%: that's $750 a month, $9,000 a year — most of it on
[regulars who already chose the place](/stories/your-regulars-are-the-expensive-part).
Across 46 restaurants, even conservative assumptions put Ogden's annual
commission bill in the hundreds of thousands.
