Ogden's food scene runs on history — a downtown built around Historic 25th
Street, where a century-old storefront is as likely to hold a taqueria as a
gastropub. What it doesn't run on, for a third of its independents, is any
way to take an online order.

In [my July catalogue](/stories/the-online-ordering-gap) of the Wasatch
Front's independents, Ogden counts 75. **33 of them — 44% — have no direct
online-ordering channel**: a website without ordering, no website at all, or
a site whose only "order" button hands the customer to a delivery app at
15–30% commission. That's better than the corridor's 53%, but it's still one
in every 2.3 restaurants.

## Established, reviewed, invisible

Ogden's version of the gap follows the corridor pattern: these are proven
kitchens, not marginal ones. Thirty of the 33 have more than a hundred
Google reviews; the group averages about 1,214 reviews at 4.4 stars. The
cuisine mix skews exactly the way the
[corridor data](/stories/the-online-ordering-gap) does — Ogden's independents
are led by 16 Mexican restaurants, with Chinese (9) and Thai (7) close
behind, the family-run kitchens least likely to have spare evenings for web
software.

## The 26 who've solved it

On the other side, 26 Ogden independents run their own ordering, and the
vendor mix mirrors [the corridor landscape](/stories/what-utah-independents-actually-use)
in miniature: Toast leads with 10, then Square, SpotOn, and Olo with 3
each. Ordering tech clearly *works* in Ogden — for the restaurants whose
volume justifies a monthly software bill. The 33 without are
disproportionately the budget spots the subscription model screens out.

## What it costs

Take a modest Ogden restaurant doing $3,000 a month through delivery apps at
25%: that's $750 a month, $9,000 a year — most of it on
[regulars who already chose the place](/stories/your-regulars-are-the-expensive-part).
Across 33 restaurants, even conservative assumptions put Ogden's annual
commission bill in the hundreds of thousands.
