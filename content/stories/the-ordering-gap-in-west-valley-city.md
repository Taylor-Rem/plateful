If you want to eat your way around the world without leaving Salt Lake
County, you go to West Valley City. Its 64 independent restaurants are the
most international lineup on the Wasatch Front: 19 Mexican kitchens — the
most concentrated anywhere in the corridor — alongside Vietnamese, Chinese,
and Salvadoran spots that people drive across the valley for.

And **32 of the 64 — exactly half — have no direct online-ordering
channel.** No ordering on the website, no website at all, or an "order"
button that hands the customer to a delivery app at
[15–30% commission](/stories/what-a-25-dollar-order-costs).

## The pattern, at its sharpest

In [the corridor-wide data](/stories/the-online-ordering-gap), one finding
stood out: the cheaper the restaurant, the bigger the gap — 66% of budget
($) independents have no direct channel, versus 44% of mid-range ($$)
spots. West Valley City is where that filter is most visible, because West
Valley is where the corridor's budget kitchens *are*. These are busy,
established places — 30 of the 32 have more than a hundred Google reviews,
averaging about 1,183 apiece — run by families who work the line themselves.
The [subscription-priced ordering tools](/stories/what-utah-independents-actually-use)
that swept the $$ dining rooms of Salt Lake and Sandy mostly never reached
9200 South and Redwood Road.

## The other 18

Eighteen West Valley independents do run their own ordering — Toast with 8,
Olo with 6 — and another 5 rely on DoorDash Storefront as their only
channel, [an arrangement with real fine print](/stories/doordash-storefront).
The tech works here just like everywhere else. It's the monthly bill that
doesn't fit a $2,000-a-month online operation.

## What half a city's gap costs

Even conservatively — say a third of the 32 sell $3,000 a month through the
apps at 25% — West Valley's kitchens send roughly **$96,000 a year** to the
marketplaces, most of it on orders from
[regulars](/stories/your-regulars-are-the-expensive-part) who already knew
exactly where they wanted to eat.
