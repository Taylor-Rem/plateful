If you want to eat your way around the world without leaving Salt Lake
County, you go to West Valley City. Its 64 independent restaurants are the
most international lineup on the Wasatch Front: 19 Mexican kitchens — the
most concentrated anywhere in the corridor — alongside Vietnamese, Chinese,
and Salvadoran spots that people drive across the valley for.

And **44 of the 64 — 69% — have no ordering channel of their own**, the
worst rate of any major city on the Front. Their online orders happen on
marketplace listings at [15–30% commission](/stories/what-a-25-dollar-order-costs)
or on [DoorDash-hosted Storefront pages](/stories/doordash-storefront)
(15 of the 44); one West Valley kitchen has no online ordering anywhere at
all, verified against every platform.

## The pattern, at its sharpest

In [the corridor-wide data](/stories/the-online-ordering-gap), one finding
stood out: the cheaper the restaurant, the bigger the gap — 72% of budget
($) independents have no channel of their own, versus 47% of mid-range ($$)
spots. West Valley City is where that filter is most visible, because West
Valley is where the corridor's budget kitchens *are*. These are busy,
established places — 39 of the 44 have more than a hundred Google reviews,
averaging over 1,050 apiece — run by families who work the line themselves.
The [subscription-priced ordering tools](/stories/what-utah-independents-actually-use)
that swept the $$ dining rooms of Salt Lake and Sandy mostly never reached
9200 South and Redwood Road.

## The other 20

Twenty West Valley independents do run their own ordering — Toast with 8,
Olo with 6 — and another 15 rely on DoorDash Storefront as their
"own" channel, [an arrangement with real fine print](/stories/doordash-storefront).
The tech works here just like everywhere else. It's the monthly bill that
doesn't fit a $2,000-a-month online operation.

## What the worst gap on the Front costs

Even conservatively — say a third of the 44 sell $3,000 a month through the
apps at 25% — West Valley's kitchens send roughly **$130,000 a year** to the
marketplaces, most of it on orders from
[regulars](/stories/your-regulars-are-the-expensive-part) who already knew
exactly where they wanted to eat.
