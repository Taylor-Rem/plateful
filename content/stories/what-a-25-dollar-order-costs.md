Here's an order everyone in Utah has placed: $25 of tacos. Same kitchen, same
food, same hungry customer a mile away. What the restaurant actually deposits
from that order depends entirely on which button the customer pushed — and the
spread is wider than most owners can quote off the top of their head.

So let's do the receipts, line by line, with sources. Every number below is
from the platforms' own published rate cards as of August 2026.

## Ground rules

Commissions apply to the **food subtotal** — the $25 — before the pile of
customer-side fees (service fees, delivery fees, small-order fees) that the
apps add on top of the check. Those customer fees are real money too; they can
add 25% or more to what your customer pays, which quietly suppresses how often
they order. But this piece is about *your* side of the receipt.

One more thing worth knowing: marketplace commissions **include** payment
processing. Nobody charges you 25% *plus* card fees — the card fees live
inside the commission. Keep that in mind when we get to Plateful's math, where
processing is a separate, visible line.

## DoorDash: $3.75 to $7.50

DoorDash sells three marketplace tiers. On our $25 order, delivered by a
Dasher:

- **Basic (15%)** — $3.75. The entry tier: your customers pay higher fees and
  you get the smallest delivery area.
- **Plus (25%)** — $6.25. Adds DashPass-member orders and a bigger radius.
- **Premier (30%)** — $7.50. The largest area, plus a "growth guarantee."

Pickup orders run 6% across all tiers. There's no monthly software fee; the
commission is the product.

## Uber Eats: $5.00 to $7.50 — and climbing

Uber Eats runs the same three-tier structure — and this March it raised the
rates. Effective March 10, 2026: the Lite tier went from 15% to **20%**,
pickup went from 6% to **7%**, and — this is the one to sit with — orders
placed by **Uber One members on the Plus tier now cost 30%** instead of 25%.

On our $25 order: **$5.00** on Lite, **$6.25** on Plus — or **$7.50** if your
customer is an Uber One subscriber — and **$7.50** on Premium.

Think about what that Uber One detail means. Uber One members are the app's
heaviest users — the people most likely to be your *regulars*. The pricing now
works like a loyalty program in reverse: the more devoted the customer, the
bigger the cut taken from their order. Uber says the increase will help it
"drive more customer demand for restaurants, courier reliability, and improved
products." Maybe so. But notice the direction rates travel. In the decade
these marketplaces have existed, the trend has only ever pointed one way.

## Plateful: $1.00

Plateful charges **4% of the food subtotal**: $1.00 on our order. Payment
processing isn't hidden inside it — your customer's card is charged through
Stripe at its standard rate (2.9% + 30¢, about $1.03 here), and the money
lands directly in your bank account; Plateful never touches it. All in,
roughly $2.03, and you keep about **$22.97 of the $25**.

Tips and tax are excluded from the 4%. Delivery, when you offer it, is quoted
from the courier network and shown to your customer at checkout — the courier
fee goes to the courier, not to Plateful. And the 4% is **capped at $399 in
any calendar month**: past roughly $10,000 in monthly online sales, every
additional order is fee-free, so your effective rate only falls as you grow.

## What it adds up to

Per-order dollars feel small until you multiply. A restaurant doing $6,000 a
month through the apps at 25% pays **$1,500 a month** — $18,000 a year. The
same volume through Plateful costs $240 a month. At $12,000 a month, the
marketplace bill hits $3,000 while Plateful's cap holds the fee at $399. That
difference is a payroll line. For some kitchens it's the margin, full stop.

None of this means the apps are useless — they surface your restaurant to
strangers, and paying for *new* customers is a legitimate marketing spend.
The expensive part, as the numbers above show, is paying marketplace rates on
the customers who already know you — [more than half of Utah's
independents](/stories/the-online-ordering-gap) have no direct channel to
send them to at all.
