Salt Lake City should be the easy case. It has the corridor's densest dining
scene, its most competitive restaurant market, and — by the numbers — the
Wasatch Front's *best* rate of direct online ordering. In
[my verified catalogue](/stories/the-online-ordering-gap) of 851 independent
restaurants, 47% of Salt Lake's 148 independents have no ordering channel of
their own, against 56% corridor-wide.

And the easy case still means **70 restaurants** — the largest absolute gap
of any city on the Front.

## Who the 70 are

They are not the strugglers. Every single one of the 70 has more than a
hundred Google reviews. As a group they average about 1,270 reviews at 4.5
stars — established, loved, busy. And to be clear about what "gap" means
after checking the platforms themselves: these restaurants *are* selling
online — through DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub listings, or through
[DoorDash-hosted Storefront pages](/stories/doordash-storefront) (20 of the
70) — they just don't own any of it. After hand-verifying every apparent
holdout, not a single Salt Lake independent is truly offline.

Salt Lake's gap is concentrated where the corridor's always is: the city's 31
independent Mexican restaurants lead the count, with deep benches of Chinese,
Thai, Vietnamese, Japanese, and Indian kitchens. The international food that
makes Salt Lake worth eating in is precisely the food you mostly can't order
directly.

## Who the other 78 use

Salt Lake is also where restaurant ordering tech is most entrenched: 78
independents run their own first-party ordering, and Toast powers 40 of
them (including six whose Toast-hosted page is their entire web presence) —
an even stronger showing than
[its 42% corridor-wide share](/stories/what-utah-independents-actually-use).
The rest scatter across Square, ChowNow, SpotOn, Olo, Clover, and a few
others. If you want to see the subscription-pricing filter at work, Salt
Lake is the cleanest example: the city with the most $$ dining rooms has
the most ordering tech, and the gap that remains sits overwhelmingly in its
budget kitchens.

## The math for one city

Put [the fee walk-through](/stories/what-a-25-dollar-order-costs) against
just this city: if only half of Salt Lake's 70 no-channel independents sell
$4,000 a month through delivery apps at 25%, that's about **$420,000 a year**
in commissions leaving the city's kitchens — for orders that are mostly
[regulars anyway](/stories/your-regulars-are-the-expensive-part).
